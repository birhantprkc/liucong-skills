# Example: thin brief → research → return → build

This is the default path when intent is already singular. The user did not name a style. Research still happens. The website is the deliverable.

## Brief (user)

> I am Lin Ke, an independent illustrator. I want a personal site so brands and art directors will commission me. Show about me, about six selected works, the collaboration process, and contact. I do not have final photos yet, and I cannot name a style.

## 1. Ingest (internal)

- Who: independent illustrator, Lin Ke.
- Why: art directors should inquire; the site must look authored, not like a marketplace profile.
- What: about, ~6 works, process, contact. Final photography missing. No style word.

No questions. The job is already a commission-led portfolio. Missing work samples are replaceable; taste will be researched.

## 2. Research

Catalog query (scenario `portfolio`, style `editorial` + `organic-craft`, component `gallery`):

- Siteinspire / Minimal Gallery for quiet editorial indexes;
- Awwwards or Godly only as contrast, not as a template;
- two live illustrator or image-led portfolios opened as direct sites.

Opened (illustrative of the method; replace with whatever the catalog and search actually return that day):

| Reference | Principle taken | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|
| A live illustrator index with large single crops and slow scroll | Let the work be the identity; captions carry title and medium | Their grid math, type, and images |
| A studio site with a short process in three numbered steps | Process as a short contract, not a blog | Their diagram language |
| A quiet about page with one portrait and a short bio | About is proof of a person, not a résumé dump | The portrait, the wording |

Observed: first viewports that convert put **one piece of work immediately**, inquiry in reach, and do not open with a hamburger of case studies.

## 3. Return (user-facing)

**Direction — editorial craft.** Paper field, the illustrations as the color system, large 4:5 crops, captions, a short process, a plain inquiry path. This matches “about / work / process / contact” and a buyer who is hiring an author, not a vendor.

**Not this:** Behance-style equal tiles, dark glass “creative studio” chrome, or a cinematic WebGL gallery that hides the images.

**Looked at:** [three URLs + one principle each]

**Will not copy:** those layouts, typefaces, and artworks.

**Will build:** one page — opening work, selected six, about, process, contact. Missing pictures of *her work*: generate six labeled illustrative placeholders at 4:5. **Materials hunted:** paper fiber, analog print texture, one licensed still life for the about crop — not CSS ornament.

Start building in the same turn after this return.

## 4. Build (after the return)

Tokens from the direction, not from the skill fixture: warm paper, ink, a muted pigment pulled from the (placeholder) work. Display serif for titles, grotesk for UI and process. Gallery is one large piece plus a caption, not six identical cards. Process is three steps with realistic duration. Contact is a real form or mailto with the right fields for a commission.

Placeholders: same 4:5 frame, print-texture, no fake client names on the pictures. Alt text says they are sample stand-ins. Replacement brief records owner, crop, and that production release waits on real files.

## 5. What this example forbids

- Asking “what style do you like?” before opening any site.
- Skipping research because the brief was short.
- Returning a week-by-week plan or a dossier YAML as the answer.
- Cloning the TRACE/WORK fixture’s evidence-rail look onto an illustrator.
- Inventing brand clients, press logos, or awards.
- Gray rectangles for the six works.
- A type-only page with no hunted paper/print materials.

## Variant: “personal blog” with no cue — lock intent first

> Make a personal blog for my friend Liu Cong. He writes notes and also wants to show projects.

“Blog” is ambiguous. **One** question, about the job, not about taste — then **stop until they answer**:

- Publishing site: notes should be readable and archivable
- Visually driven site: projects and presence first
- I have a reference: next message is a URL or screenshot

If they pick publishing, research good publications and build a small magazine of notes plus a short project shelf. If they pick visual, research high-craft personal sites, not blog themes.

They brought no brand. That is fine: invent the system from the lock + notes / projects. Do not stall. Do not keep asking.

## Variant: “company website” — same rule, different options

> Build a company website. Make it look premium.

Do not research “corporate websites” yet. **One** question, then wait:

- Product site: demo, features, conversion
- Company intro: business lines, trust, contact
- Recruiting: team and roles
- I have a reference

Then research that job only.

## Variant: taste cue, no brand, no question

> Personal site for Jia — restrained, wood and paper. Show work and writing.

The material cue plus both content types already lock a quiet mixed site. Research that. Hunt wood, washi, ink — do not draw concentric SVG rings and call them growth rings. Build. Do not ask.

## Variant: user already has a reference

> I like this site [URL or screenshot]. Make my portfolio from these three images.

**Do:** that URL/screenshot is reference #1. Extract principles. Open one or two other high-craft peers. Build. No direction question.

**Do not:** ignore the reference and start a generic search; pixel-clone the signature layout; ask them to describe the screenshot.
