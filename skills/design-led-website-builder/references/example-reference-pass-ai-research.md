# AI research product reference pass

This is a dense product-site comparison, not the default thin-brief path. For the default order (brief → research → return → build) see [thin brief, illustrator](example-thin-brief.md). This example still matters: catalog routing must become evidence-calibrated observations from public pages.

This worked example turns catalog routing into direct evidence for the fictional brief in [the AI research product dossier](example-dossier-ai-research.yaml). It is a reference pass, not a forensic reproduction: the goal is to compare product communication, interface proof, visual tone, and implementation signals closely enough to change a design decision.

## Scope and method

- Reviewed: 2026-08-25.
- State: public, signed-out home pages at 1280×720.
- Targets: [Elicit](https://elicit.com/), [Consensus](https://consensus.app/), and [Scite](https://scite.ai/).
- Surfaces: semantic DOM, computed styles, interactive-element inventory, page assets, and public official documentation where useful.
- Not exercised: authenticated workflows, submitted searches, mobile layouts, assistive-technology walkthroughs, or asset licensing.

The evidence labels follow this Skill's protocol. Short product phrases are included only to identify the observed state; all design conclusions are original synthesis.

## Elicit

### Observed structure and interaction

The public page has a semantic banner, main region, and footer. Its opening states the research category and value, then provides a video with a visible pause control and text that explains the report workflow. Product captures have descriptive alternatives rather than decorative filenames. Trust logos, feature routes, explicit accuracy and transparency material, case evidence, release notes, and FAQ extend the same argument.

At the reviewed viewport the display heading was centered, approximately 64px with a 76.8px line height, in a custom editorial face. The page used a warm off-white background and dark teal headline color. The rendered state contained 23 images, one video, 44 inline SVG elements, nine buttons, 60 links, and no canvas.

### Detected implementation signals

The asset inventory contained 103 resources: 72 scripts, five font files, 19 images, one video, and six other resources. Framer-hosted media and script names associated with Framer, React, and Motion were directly present. The hero video was a WebM asset. These signals support a **detected** Framer/React/Motion delivery stack for this page; they do not establish exact package versions or prove how every animation is authored.

### Decision for the new site

Keep the dossier's editorial-instrument direction, but move product truth ahead of atmosphere. A real workflow view, transcript or ordered explanation, and explicit controls should do more expressive work than gradients or generic research imagery.

## Consensus

### Observed structure and interaction

The public URL resolved to an application-like page. A left rail and main task area placed the research-question field, source/corpus options, a deeper-search mode, filters, and submit action above publisher and university proof. Later sections demonstrated literature review, medical research, natural-language filtering, and agreement analysis with example prompts.

At the reviewed viewport the active element was the research textarea. The page used a dense sans-serif hierarchy: approximately 28px for the main task heading and 16–18px for many explanatory headings. The rendered state contained 25 buttons, 53 inline SVG elements, two images, no video, and no canvas. The raw input count was high because the application shell included internal or non-visible controls; it should not be interpreted as visible interface density without a visibility filter.

### Detected implementation signals

The asset inventory contained 68 resources: 29 scripts, four font files, seven images, two stylesheets, and 26 other resources. Next.js page-router chunks and webpack assets were directly present, as were CircularXX webfonts and Clerk authentication resources. Product and plan endpoints also appeared in the page traffic. This supports **detected** Next.js and Clerk use in the reviewed state. Analytics and monitoring resources were also present, but they are operational observations, not design recommendations.

Navigation reached a usable DOM even though the browser's load wait timed out. That timeout is a limitation of the probe, not evidence of a broken page.

### Decision for the new site

Treat the product task as the strongest possible proof. If a safe public demo is unavailable, use a truthful, non-submitting fixture with clear boundaries rather than a fake live field. Pair modes and filters with user questions, not with implementation jargon.

## Scite

### Observed structure and interaction

The public page opens with a skip link, navigation, a concise research proposition, and a real question field. A compact evidence layer follows before the longer feature story. Subsequent sections explain differentiation through product-specific illustrations and screenshots, followed by testimonials, a closing action, and an accessibility link.

At the reviewed viewport the main heading was approximately 42px/600 weight and the search field occupied about 688×39px. The rendered state contained 53 images, 19 inline SVG elements, one canvas, 11 buttons, 30 links, and no video. The mere presence of one canvas does not establish that the visible experience depends on WebGL or 3D.

### Detected implementation signals

The asset inventory contained 106 resources: 40 scripts, 37 images, nine stylesheets, and 20 other resources. A client root, hashed bundles, and loadable-chunk markers were directly present. They support a **detected** code-split client application. The precise framework is **unknown** from the collected signals; React would be an inference, not a finding.

### Decision for the new site

Introduce a short evidence contract before the visitor reaches a long feature list: what is searched, what can be traced, what remains uncertain, and where human review enters. Use original diagrams or approved product captures to prove those statements.

## Cross-reference synthesis

The three sites differ in surface treatment, but converge on six useful rules for this brief:

1. Name the research job immediately; do not make the visitor decode an abstract AI category.
2. Let a genuine product task, workflow, or output carry the opening proof.
3. Use example questions and product states to explain capability in user language.
4. Put provenance, verification, or methodology close to the first claimed output.
5. Connect trust claims to inspectable validation, cases, or documented methods.
6. Separate product truth from decorative atmosphere so the page remains persuasive without motion or proprietary media.

This comparison strengthens the fictional direction but does not license a composite clone. The new site should preserve its own content order, visual system, diagrams, copy, and interaction model.

## Remaining uncertainty

- The Elicit screenshot probe failed, so its visual notes rely on DOM, computed styles, and assets rather than a captured frame.
- No search was submitted and no authenticated product flow was reviewed.
- Mobile composition, responsive media behavior, keyboard traversal, reduced motion, and screen-reader behavior of the reference sites were not tested.
- Detected libraries are page-state evidence, not versioned dependency manifests or proof of effect ownership.
- Reference media, fonts, logos, interface captures, and product claims remain unavailable for reuse unless separately approved.
